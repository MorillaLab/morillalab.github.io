The shape of data, from lungs to leaves.

Morilla Lab builds topological and geometric machine learning for multi-omics data, and puts it to work on human disease and plant biology.

Machine Learning in Multi-Omics (MLiMO), in Málaga at IHSM La Mayora (UMA–CSIC) and in Paris at LAGA (CNRS, Université Sorbonne Paris Nord).

Explore our research Open positions

Topology and machine learning

Persistent homology, manifold learning and optimal transport describe the shape of data in ways that survive noise and changes of scale. We build them into transformers, graph networks and autoencoders, and into privacy-preserving federated learning across hospitals.

Projects: TaelCore, GeoTop, TopoAttention, Polygenic Deep Transport. Preprint: PTOPOFL. Or try persistent homology in your browser.

Human disease

With clinical partners we model patient data to predict outcomes and explain disease: mortality risk after lung transplantation, COVID-19 severity from the plasma proteome, treatment response in acute severe ulcerative colitis, the evolution of chronic lymphocytic leukaemia and its bone-marrow microenvironment, and polygenic inheritance in cancer, inflammatory and cardiovascular disease.

Projects: TopoAttention, TaelCore, MMD-LLC, Polygenic Deep Transport.

Plant biology

At IHSM La Mayora we bring the same methods to plants: a cell-type-annotated spatial atlas of tomato leaves infected by tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV), prediction of small signalling peptides with S²-PepAnalyst, and multimodal models of how tomato roots integrate signals from fungal symbionts.

Projects: tylcv-tomato-visium, PlantAG, S²-PepAnalyst.

  • Polygenic Deep Transport

    2024–present

    Human diseaseTopology & ML

    Topological deep learning and optimal transport to study polygenic inheritance in cancer, inflammatory and cardiovascular disease. Its code, PolyCLIP-T, combines contrastive learning with topology to find coherent groups of low-penetrance variants in families with multimorbid disorders.

  • PlantAG

    2024–present

    Plant biologyTopology & ML

    Plant Tissue Annotation via Graph Topology. It assigns cell types to plant spatial-transcriptomics spots without a single-cell reference, describing each spot by the shape of the expression landscape around it (persistent homology) and learning the types with a graph convolutional network. Built for the tylcv-tomato-visium atlas.

    CodeZenodo archive

Persistent homology, live

Much of our work starts from one idea. Join points that are closer than a scale ε, fill in triangles, and watch which connected components and loops appear and disappear as ε grows.

The barcode records each one as a bar. Long bars tend to be real structure; short bars, noise. Drag the slider, or press Play.

Ian Morilla

Principal investigator

Ian Morilla leads MLiMO at IHSM La Mayora (UMA–CSIC, Málaga) and holds a research position at LAGA, Institut Galilée (CNRS, Université Sorbonne Paris Nord). His work connects topological data analysis, deep learning and integrative multi-omics, in human disease and in plant biology. He has authored more than 27 peer-reviewed articles and is a co-inventor on US patent filings for AI-based prediction of treatment response.
