Morilla Lab

Machine Learning in Multi-Omics (MLiMO)

We build machine-learning methods that read the shape of biological data, with topology, deep learning and optimal transport, and we apply them to human disease and plant biology: from lung-transplant outcomes and leukaemia to tomato viruses and plant signalling peptides.

Based in Málaga at IHSM La Mayora (UMA–CSIC) and in Paris at LAGA (CNRS, Université Sorbonne Paris Nord).

Research

Our methods come from topology and machine learning; our questions come from medicine and plant science. The same tools travel between the two.

Topology and geometry for machine learning

Persistent homology, manifold learning and optimal transport describe the shape of data in ways that survive noise and changes of scale. We turn those descriptions into features and layers for deep-learning models, such as transformers, graph networks and autoencoders, and into privacy-preserving federated learning across hospitals.

Projects: TaelCore, GeoTop, TopoAttention, Polygenic Deep Transport. Preprint: PTOPOFL.

Human disease and clinical prediction

With clinical partners we model patient data to predict outcomes and explain disease: mortality risk after lung transplantation, COVID-19 severity from the plasma proteome, treatment response in acute severe ulcerative colitis, the evolution of chronic lymphocytic leukaemia and its bone-marrow microenvironment, and polygenic inheritance in cancer, inflammatory and cardiovascular disease.

Projects: TopoAttention, TaelCore, MMD-LLC, Polygenic Deep Transport.

Plant biology and plant–virus interactions

At IHSM La Mayora we bring the same methods to plants: a cell-type-annotated spatial atlas of tomato leaves infected by tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV), prediction of small signalling peptides with S²-PepAnalyst, and multimodal models of how tomato roots integrate signals from fungal symbionts.

Projects: Topo-Spatial-Transcriptomics-TYLCV, S²-PepAnalyst.

More open-source repositories (7)
  • PlantAG: automated cell-type annotation for plant spatial transcriptomics with no reference atlas, coupling Vietoris–Rips persistent homology with a graph convolutional network.
  • PhytoPepBERT: single-sequence language modelling that learns family-defining structure in plant signalling peptides.
  • PolyCLIP-T: contrastive, CLIP-like learning to find low-penetrance polygenic variants in complex diseases.
  • TopoGCN-LT-Insights: graph convolutional networks aided by topological data analysis, for lung-transplantation outcomes.
  • RL-TBoost: reinforcement learning enhanced by topological data analysis, to predict one-year mortality after lung transplantation.
  • Genetic-Bell: a non-linear model of epistasis.
  • multimorbidity-SEM: structural equation modelling of the links between women’s cancers and cardiovascular and chronic inflammatory diseases.

Ian Morilla

Principal investigator

Ian Morilla leads MLiMO at IHSM La Mayora (UMA–CSIC, Málaga) and holds a research position at LAGA, Institut Galilée (CNRS, Université Sorbonne Paris Nord). His work connects topological data analysis, deep learning and integrative multi-omics, in human disease and in plant biology. He has authored more than 27 peer-reviewed articles and is a co-inventor on US patent filings for AI-based prediction of treatment response.
